# VEVOR ZX31006 sump basin: preparation and installation guideline

For an outdoor roof-water sump in clay that holds its shape but deforms under a person's weight. Existing hole: approximately 30 inches wide × 54 inches deep.

## Dimensions and planning

VEVOR's current product dimension image specifies a basin diameter of 21.26 inches (540 mm) and height to lid of 31.50 inches (800 mm), excluding discharge piping above the lid. These replace the earlier nominal 18 × 30-inch figures. Check the delivered unit; if its actual outside measurement is larger, use that measurement for clearance.

The installation manual calls for a hole approximately 24 inches wider in diameter and 12 inches deeper than the basin. It specifies 1/8–3/4-inch pea gravel or crushed stone for the sub-base and backfill.

## Before starting

1. Have a person competent in excavation safety assess the recently wet, 4.5-foot-deep hole before anyone enters or widens it. A hole less than 5 feet deep can still require collapse protection. Keep people away from unsupported edges.
2. Resolve the insufficient width. The approximately 46-inch dimension provides installation clearance; it is not a safe-slope or shoring design. Safe excavation may require additional width.
3. Have the approximately 22.5-inch replacement foundation over soft clay specified locally, including its placement and consolidation method. Hand seating of stone alone does not verify adequate bearing capacity or acceptable settlement.
4. Check groundwater and anti-flotation requirements for the basin. Stone bedding and backfill alone do not establish that an empty basin is adequately anchored.

## Prepare the bottom

1. Bail or pump out standing water from above where possible. Keep electrical connections dry and use suitable electrical protection. Do not use the kit pump to ingest mud or stone.
2. Remove loose slurry, roots, debris, and disturbed clay. Preserve intact clay underneath. Avoid walking on or pounding wet clay. Do not keep digging deeper solely because intact clay is moist.
3. If water continually returns, arrange suitable water control before placement. Avoid washing soil out from beneath the excavation or adjacent structures.
4. Use permeable construction geotextile selected by a civil/drainage supplier for separation and stabilization beneath aggregate on soft clay. Cover the bottom, turn it up the soil-facing sides, and follow the supplier's overlap and placement instructions. Do not substitute plastic or ordinary weed-control fabric.

Fabric helps prevent clay and aggregate from mixing; it does not prevent deeper clay from compressing.

### Lower-cost separator option

Base-only geotextile is a compromise: it separates the bottom, but clay can still migrate into the side backfill. Full bottom-and-side coverage provides more complete separation. Confirm that the chosen fabric and arrangement suit the soil before installation.

- Cover the entire excavation bottom, not just the basin footprint.
- Turn the fabric up around the bottom perimeter, following the supplier's placement requirements. Place stone gently without tearing or displacing it.
- For a 46-inch-wide hole, a 6 × 6-foot piece provides approximately 13 inches of upturn on each side on an ideal flat section. Allow more for irregular ground, folds, and the supplier's requirements.
- Ask a drainage supplier or contractor for an unused construction-geotextile remnant. Do not substitute cardboard, burlap, plastic, tarps, or ordinary weed-control fabric.
- The previously suggested 6-oz fabric is a shopping option, not a verified minimum specification. Fabric weight alone does not establish suitability.

## Place and consolidate the base

1. Use clean, washed, angular crushed stone within VEVOR's specified 1/8–3/4-inch range. Avoid rubble, oversized sharp rocks, dirt, and fines. Crushed stone is preferred in this guideline; VEVOR also permits pea gravel.
2. Spread the first layer gently across the entire bottom without puncturing the fabric. Do not dump the full foundation thickness at once.
3. Subject to the locally specified foundation method, place roughly 4-inch loose layers. This is a general placement suggestion, not a manufacturer-specified compaction schedule or a proven first-layer stabilization thickness for soft clay.
4. For gentle hand seating, use a broad, flat-bottom hand tamper, typically about 8 × 8 inches. Lift it a few inches and use controlled, light strokes in overlapping passes. Do not forcefully pound stone into the clay. Operate only with safe access; do not enter an unsupported hole to tamp.
5. Rake down high spots, fill low spots, and repeat light passes until little additional stone rearrangement is visible. This indicates seating of the stone, not verified foundation compaction or long-term settlement performance.
6. Check elevation before adding each layer. Continue only while the underlying foundation remains stable.
7. Do not flood or jet the stone to settle it. Do not use a jumping-jack compactor on this yielding wet-clay foundation. Have any mechanical compaction method selected for the actual soil, aggregate, and excavation conditions.

STOP if stone keeps sinking, the surface feels springy, adjacent areas heave, clay squeezes upward, or muddy water pumps through the aggregate. Do not add more stone or tamp harder to conceal movement. Have stabilization, replacement, or another support solution specified before proceeding.

## Set the basin and connect pipes

1. Finish the base level at the calculated elevation, supporting the entire basin bottom continuously. Do not support the basin on isolated bricks or blocks.
2. Set the basin upright and level. Confirm that it does not rock and that its lid remains accessible at the intended finished elevation.
3. Connect inlet pipes with suitable sealed fittings. The manual describes a 4-inch inlet and approximately 1/4 inch per foot fall toward the basin. Support pipes independently so their weight does not pull on the basin fittings.
4. Follow the supplied manual for discharge piping, check valve, venting, electrical connections, and float clearance. Keep stone outside the basin and the interior clear for the pump and float.
5. For roof-water collection alone, do not drill groundwater collection holes unless the system is also designed to receive groundwater.

## Backfill and finish

1. Place approved aggregate evenly around the basin in shallow layers, gently settling each and checking that the basin stays level and round. Avoid heavy pressure or mechanical compaction against its plastic walls.
2. Keep excavated clay out of the aggregate envelope. Maintain geotextile separation from surrounding soil.
3. For lawn areas, where permitted by the basin requirements, separate upper aggregate from the soil finish with geotextile and provide a soil cap. Grade away from the lid, avoid a depression collecting muddy runoff, and keep the lid accessible.
4. For paving or concrete, provide separately designed support and a cover rated for the expected loading. Keep removable lid fasteners accessible.
5. Discharge to a suitable outlet away from the pit. Stone surrounded by clay is not a dependable soakaway.
